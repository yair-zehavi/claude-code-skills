# Direction library

Media that resist the AI look, because their whole character is surface and
imperfection. Each entry gives: what it is, **what to name in the prompt** (the
load-bearing detail), palette discipline, when to reach for it, and where it fails.

Pick ONE per graphic. Mixing two produces mush. Show a client two or three, never five.

---

## 1. Cut-paper collage (Matisse découpage)
Torn and scissor-cut matte paper layered on a paper ground, shot flat under raking light.
**Name:** genuine torn paper fibre at every edge, hand-cut irregularity, each layer casting
its own soft real drop shadow, slight buckling under the light, cotton fibre tooth in the ground.
**Palette:** 4-5 papers, no more. **Good for:** warm, tactile, family-facing; large flat
fields take type beautifully; prints superbly. **Fails when:** the subject is built from
too many overlapping shapes and stops reading as an object - insist on ONE bold silhouette.

## 2. Four-colour silkscreen travel poster
1950s ocean-liner / airline poster. Cassandre, Villemot. Bold flat geometric simplification.
**Name:** coarse halftone dot texture inside the flat fields, ink misregistration leaving a
1mm colour offset at the edges, ink bleeding into the paper fibre, mottled ageing, completely
flat lighting with no gradients.
**Palette:** exactly four inks, one of which is the paper. **Good for:** anything with travel,
logistics, movement, arrival; it is a poster already, so print layout is native. Highest
"would a premium brand post this" score of the library. **Fails when:** you ask for realism
in the same prompt - it goes half-rendered and loses the ink.

## 3. Synthetic cubism / papiers collés
Picasso 1912-1921, Braque. Large flat interlocking planes, several viewpoints at once.
**Name:** thick confident hand-drawn black contour lines that deliberately slip out of
register with the colour beneath, dry-brush drag, gouache pooling at the edge of each shape,
exposed canvas weave, pencil under-drawing showing through, torn kraft collaged into planes.
**Palette:** five values max, high contrast, and say **"no muddy mid browns, no beige wash"** -
without that it drifts into the drab analytic-cubism brown that reads as a mess.
**Good for:** the boldest, most "artistic" option; great when the client wants to stand out.
**Fails when:** the client's floor is "clever and brand-relevant" - cubism buys the art and
can lose the read. Offer it as a wildcard, not the lead.

## 4. Swiss International Style / Bauhaus flat vector
Absolute flat geometry, extreme restraint, enormous negative space.
**Name:** no shading, no highlights, no perspective; the faintest risograph offset grain;
one small black element as counterweight.
**Palette:** three plus one tiny accent. **Good for:** the most "designed" and least AI-looking
of all; ideal when the copy is long and needs room. **Fails when:** the occasion wants warmth -
it reads cold. Wrong for a family holiday unless paired with a warm ground.

## 5. Papercut, ketubah / mizrach tradition
Fine hand-cut gold paper over a deep coloured ground, raking light.
**Name:** the paper physically lifting off the backing and casting delicate shadows, real
thickness at every edge, tiny asymmetries left by the hand cutting.
**Composition:** insist the arched opening is **wide and generous, plain and unornamented**,
occupying the whole central area - otherwise the ornament fills the frame and there is
nowhere for type to sit.
**Palette:** gold on one deep colour. **Good for:** Jewish and religious occasions; a genuine
craft tradition, so it reads authentic rather than generated. Hide brand objects in the
filigree. **Fails when:** overused - it is the default "elegant" lane and gets stale fast.

## 6. Linocut / woodcut relief print
**Name:** the carved gouge marks left visible in the block, chatter at the edge of every cut,
ink pooling unevenly where the block pressed harder, one colour slightly out of register,
printed on kraft or newsprint with the fibre showing.
**Palette:** two inks. **Good for:** harvest, agriculture, earthy and rustic occasions, food
clients. Very cheap to print. **Fails when:** the design needs fine detail - relief printing
cannot hold it, and asking for it produces a fake.

## 7. Risograph
**Name:** two or three spot inks overprinting with visible misregistration, the fluorescent
pink and teal that only riso makes, heavy grain, ink that sits ON the paper, and multiply
overprint where the two inks cross to make a third colour.
**Palette:** name the two inks; the third colour must come from the overlap.
**Good for:** young, cultural, editorial, event and community clients. Modern and fashionable.
**Fails when:** the client is conservative or the piece must feel expensive.

## 8. Letterpress on cotton rag
**Name:** deep debossed impression pressing into thick cotton rag paper, ink squeeze building
at the edge of every letterform and rule, blind emboss where there is no ink, deckled edge,
photographed with raking light so the impression casts its own shadow.
**Palette:** one or two inks plus blind. **Good for:** invitations, premium print, law/finance/
luxury clients, anything that must feel expensive in the hand. **Fails when:** it is a screen
deliverable - the whole point is physical and it flattens out on Instagram.

## 9. Azulejo / hand-painted ceramic tile
**Name:** hand-painted cobalt on tin-glazed white earthenware, brush strokes visible in the
glaze, crazing in the glaze surface, slight bleed of the pigment under the glaze, grout lines
between tiles, one chipped corner.
**Palette:** cobalt and white plus one. **Good for:** Mediterranean, hospitality,
heritage clients. Instantly local and instantly not-AI. **Fails when:** the motif needs to be
modern - the medium drags everything traditional.

## 10. Embroidery / needlepoint
**Name:** individual threads catching the light, satin stitch and french knots, the weave of
the canvas showing through between stitches, slight puckering where the thread pulls, a loose
thread end.
**Palette:** limited floss colours. **Good for:** warmth, family, craft, textile and homeware
clients; extremely hard to read as AI. **Fails when:** the subject needs hard geometry.

## 11. Stained glass / leaded light
**Name:** lead cames between every colour field, the texture and slight bubbles inside the
glass itself, light transmitted THROUGH the glass rather than reflected off it, colour
pooling on the surface below.
**Palette:** jewel colours on black lead. **Good for:** synagogue and church occasions,
memorials, anything solemn and beautiful. **Fails when:** used for something light-hearted.

## 12. Mid-century children's book gouache
Provensen, Blegvad, Alice and Martin. Flat gouache, charmingly naive drawing.
**Name:** flat matte gouache with visible brush texture, slightly wobbly hand-drawn line,
colours printed slightly off from the line work, aged paper.
**Palette:** four to five. **Good for:** family, education, anything for children, warm and
nostalgic occasions. **Fails when:** the client wants to look serious or premium.

## 13. Copperplate engraving, hand-coloured
**Name:** fine engraved hatching and cross-hatching building the tone, the plate mark
impressed into the paper, delicate hand-applied watercolour washes sitting slightly outside
the lines, foxing and age-toning on laid paper.
**Palette:** ink black plus two washes. **Good for:** botanical subjects, heritage, wine and
spirits, apothecary, anything that wants gravitas. **Fails when:** it needs to feel current.

## 14. Constructivist photomontage
Rodchenko, Lissitzky. Hard diagonals, cut halftone photo fragments, bold slab type shapes.
**Name:** coarse newspaper halftone in the photographic fragments, hard scissor-cut edges,
strong diagonal axis, flat blocks of red and black on cream.
**Palette:** red, black, cream. Strictly. **Good for:** bold announcements, campaigns, anything
that must shout without being cheap. **Fails when:** the occasion is tender.

## 15. Ink brush with a single seal
**Name:** one confident loaded brush gesture, dry-brush splitting at the end of the stroke,
ink bleeding softly into absorbent paper, enormous empty space, a single small vermilion seal.
**Palette:** black, paper, one red seal. **Good for:** restraint, minimalism, condolence and
solemn occasions, luxury. **Fails when:** the client wants "festive".

## 16. Terrazzo / mosaic chip
Mid-century terrazzo floors, or Roman mosaic.
**Name:** irregular stone and glass chips set in a cementitious ground, the grout and setting
bed visible between chips, chips catching light at slightly different angles, a worn polished
surface.
**Palette:** the chip colours, three or four. **Good for:** mid-century nostalgia, construction and
property clients, municipal work. **Fails when:** the image needs fine figuration.

## 17. Hard enamel pin / cloisonné
**Name:** raised polished metal lines separating every colour field, glossy hard enamel filling
each cell and sitting flush with the metal, the metal catching a specular highlight along its
edge, on a plain solid ground.
**Palette:** four enamels plus gold. **Good for:** merch-adjacent, playful-but-premium, tech and
startup clients. **Fails when:** the design has soft or organic edges.

## 18. Kilim / flat-woven textile
**Name:** the warp and weft visible as stepped diagonal edges wherever a shape is not
horizontal, wool fibre texture, slight irregularity in the row spacing, fringe at one edge.
**Palette:** natural dye colours, four to five. **Good for:** Middle-Eastern heritage, home,
hospitality. **Fails when:** curves matter - a loom cannot make them, and forcing it looks fake.

## 19. Cyanotype / technical blueprint
**Name:** Prussian blue ground with the drawing in white line, dimension arrows and leader
lines, a title block ruled in the corner, uneven coating brush marks at the paper edges,
slight fading and water staining.
**Palette:** one blue plus white. **Good for:** engineering, construction, logistics, moving,
architecture. The medium IS the client's own document language. **Fails when:** warmth is wanted.

## 20. Vintage crate label / matchbox
**Name:** small-format commercial lithography, ornate scrolled border, spot colours slightly
out of register, worn and scuffed edges, a gold foil block, the paper yellowed unevenly.
**Palette:** four spot colours plus gold. **Good for:** food and produce, delis, wine, anything
with heritage or a founding date. **Fails when:** the brand is modern and minimal.

## Choosing

Ask three questions:
1. **What is the occasion's emotional register?** Solemn kills 12, 17, 20. Festive kills 15.
2. **What does the client physically make or move?** Their own material world often names the
   medium - a moving company gets the blueprint (19); a food client gets the crate label (20);
   a textile client gets the weave (18).
3. **How much copy has to fit?** Long copy needs 4, 1 or 2 (big flat fields). Short copy can
   afford 3, 9, 11, 16.

Then: keep the campaign in ONE palette across the options, so the client is choosing a
medium, not a colour scheme.
